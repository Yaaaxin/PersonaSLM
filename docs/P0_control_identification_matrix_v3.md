## Family-specific judgments from inspection

These are automated analytical assessments, not human annotations or PASS labels for semantic validity.

**N_style:** the six selected controls are single generic declarative sentences with no imperative, named city, explicit personal trait or repeated sentence. This removes the old repeated document-format padding. It does not create a style-free string: the unchanged `Speaker style profile:` heading invites a profile interpretation, and the sentences exemplify a complete, concise, relatively formal expository register. This is especially material for Riley/Robin (brevity/single-sentence interventions) and Samara (formality). It is a plausible candidate for a *specified replacement comparison*, pending actual review; it is not yet an accepted neutral-style placebo.

**History A:** all six use the same general question, `What does a conversation involve?`, with one of two relevant-to-that-question generic replies. This preserves a question–answer exchange, role ordering, speaker name, date syntax and total tokens. It removes the old residence proposition and its internal past reference, replaces residence relevance with discourse content, and changes deictic/personal wording. It supports a format-and-length-controlled comparison only if that phrase means holding format/length constant while replacing content. It does **not** estimate the effect of historical formatting.

**History B:** all six preserve `Where were you living back then?`. Riley, Samara and Tessa respond `The city or the address?`; Devon, Blair and Robin respond `Are you asking about the city?`. The first subgroup adds an address-versus-city distinction that did not occur in the active reply. The second uses second-person wording aimed at the interlocutor, not a new claim about the target's residence. None asserts the required current answer, but all turn question–answer into question–clarification and can prime questioning, uncertainty or non-answering. Residence-topic alignment is achieved in a limited sense; semantic/speech-act equivalence is not.

**History C:** York, Bath, Exeter, Hull, Carlisle and Gloucester are removed from the respective replies and replaced by `a city`. The place category, residence relation, target attribution, historical question and dated wrapper are retained. For Riley, Samara and Tessa, `at the time` also becomes `back then`; for the other three it remains `at the time`. The exact geographic identity and named alternative-answer competitor disappear, while a coarse past personal fact remains. This is a diagnostic information-generalization intervention. Calling it neutral or a pure temporal-conflict manipulation would be incorrect.

Named evidence/questions remove the old “the person” versus Alice ambiguity in these new cases. They do not establish real-speaker simulation: answering *about* a named person in an Assistant turn is not independently verified to be speaking *as* that person. The v2 fixtures do not instantiate the F factor or a complete factorial pilot. The six speakers/styles and one question type are development coverage, not population sampling or a scientific sample size.
