# P1c Scientific Identifiability Resolution — Review v1

Maintainer: Yaxin ZHENG. Paper: *Understanding the Effects of Persona Conditioning on Dialogue Reliability in Small Language Models*. Target: PLOS ONE. This is prospective analysis of unchanged development stimuli, not approval or an experimental result. Protocol v1.1 remains the historical authority; v1.2 Revised Proposed r1 is not adopted.

## Decision and evidence

The existing bank supports **operational auxiliary-history replacement contrasts**, subject to researcher review. It does **not** separately identify a pure relevance mechanism, a pure temporal-status mechanism, or stale-answer competition. The four cells are independently routed, but their semantic ingredients are not independently isolated. A numerical R×T interaction would describe the interaction of these specific replacement packages.

No concrete ground-truth or routing defect requiring stimulus correction was found. No case, history, prompt, condition ID, order or configuration was changed. Scientific usefulness still requires an explicit judgment about the narrower question; a technically valid prompt is insufficient. Execution remains **NO-GO pending scientific approval and authorization**.

The [audit](validation/p1c/scientific_review_v1/audit_summary_v1.json) covers 12 cases, six speakers, 48 R/T histories, 12 neutral histories and 12 empty-history prompts. These are 12 paired cases sharing six speaker/ledger clusters, not 12 independent speakers. All 72 complete prompts were retokenized with the pinned native vocabulary-only tool. No generation was performed.

## Topical relevance is not answer competition

R1 repeats a **parcel label**; R0 repeats a **music label**. Questions request a collection desk, desk plus service day, or destination room. None of the 48 active histories contains a required answer component or a source fact used by the reference derivation. Deleting or symbolically substituting auxiliary facts in an internal solver copy leaves all 12 references unchanged. Removing a required fact breaks reference answerability. These are offline dependency checks, not altered Track A inputs and not evidence that a model cannot exploit shortcuts.

R0 remains about the same speaker: it is unrelated to the requested property, not irrelevant in every personal or entity sense. Only four route questions explicitly contain “parcel”; the other eight depend on the intended collection scenario for topical relatedness. Researcher review must confirm that this is a meaningful, sufficiently consistent distinction. A null auxiliary contrast cannot falsify direct old-answer competition.

## Counterfactual limitations requiring acknowledgment

All 24 within-R T pairs change both the color and interval; none preserves the same full entity/property/value proposition. All 24 within-T R pairs change the predicate and color. Both interventions repeat facts already in the **same** trusted ledger; evidence availability is fixed, but repetition and salience vary.

There is a systematic cross-topic color relation: in every historical case, R1T1 and R0T0 share a color; in every current case, R0T1 and R1T0 share a color. The cyclic construction also links colors and schedule periods. These 12 off-diagonal equalities are not proof of answer leakage, but permit lexical/period cues that could contribute to an interaction. Date-token overlap also varies: T1's interval begins in the requested month, whereas T0's does not. Color, interval position, elapsed time and date similarity cannot be separated here.

All histories use the same archive date, Archivist question, named speaker and source framing. Active replies are positive assertions without explicit negation. The archive date falls **after** the requested historical date in six cases. These are retrospective historical-state questions with a supplied schedule, not a study of knowledge available at that past time. In current cases, the schedule is known before the requested day. If the researcher intended contemporaneous knowledge restrictions, this bank would not answer that question and would need a separately approved revision.

Task family is nested within speaker; two cases share each ledger; question wording and property values have limited variation. No within-family comparison can establish population generality. The question's date selects an interval, so a universally newest-value strategy is invalid for all six historical cases. Multiple route destinations make a unique-terminal shortcut insufficient, but correct answers would still not prove a particular reasoning process.

## Minimal resolution and prospective alternative

Recommend retaining the 72 proposed inputs and leading interpretation with the **four fixed-factor simple comparisons**. Report average R/T and R×T as secondary operational summaries, retaining all signs, cancellation and unknown bounds. This is a prospective reporting recommendation, not a silent replacement of the earlier unapproved endpoint hierarchy. It needs researcher confirmation.

If the scientific target is specifically stale-answer competition, a **separate future proposal** should compare a historically true expired target-property assertion with a historically true expired non-answer assertion, both at T0, while preserving the ledger, question and requested date. Match length/format and independently review whether the target assertion is a plausible competing answer. Include the same shorter/no-history and reviewed-neutral roles as justified; do not call it a fully orthogonal R×T design. A valid target-history arm would additionally repeat the correct answer and would require explicit repetition controls. No such cases, new condition IDs or requests were added here.

This limited calibration can inform measurement feasibility and whether a scoped replacement effect merits independent study. It cannot establish what caused P1b's H observations, validate a new algorithm, or supply confirmatory evidence.
