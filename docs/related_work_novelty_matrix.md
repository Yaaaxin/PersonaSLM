## Overlap and gaps requiring further verification

These are **our inferences about design coverage**, not claims that the authors proved a gap or that no other work covers it.

| ID | Overlap with PersonaSLM | Candidate unresolved issue for this project |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Profile-conditioned dialogue | Fictional profile consistency differs from faithful simulation of an actual speaker under shared dated evidence |
| W2 | Persona facts versus response truth | Separating style, extra facts and history at inference without learning a new algorithm |
| W3 | User histories, personalization and small models | Task-specific personalization gains differ from independently measured dialogue reliability and fidelity |
| W4 | Direct persona/accuracy evaluation, including 3B models | Real-speaker linguistic/history interventions and validated semantic placebos; broad persona-performance novelty is already occupied |
| W5 | Persona fidelity and multidimensional evaluation | Separate fidelity from trusted-evidence truth rather than treating persona consistency as factual reliability |
| W6 | Temporal conflict, updates and retrieval/utilization | Shared-evidence Track A identification and controlled S/F/H effects versus missing-memory explanations |
| W7 | Context competition and position controls | Determine whether a persona-specific residual survives strong length/position/content controls |
| W8 | Simulation of real individuals | Dated evidence reliability during dialogue, distinct from predicting attitudes or surveys |
| W9 | Personalization interference and mechanisms | Factual/temporal dialogue endpoints differ from safety; “personal context can cause harm” is not a novel generic claim |

## Falsifiable hypothesis proposals

All five require researcher-approved designs, new development/confirmation separation and a predeclared practically meaningful effect bound. “No significant difference” alone does not falsify an effect; adequate precision or an equivalence design is needed. Compute estimates below are proposed counts, not executed workloads. Hardware/model labels alone provide no novelty.

| Hypothesis | Existing coverage / remaining uncertainty | Required experiment and possible supporting result | Possible falsifying result | Confounds and cost | Novelty risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1: Part of an apparent style effect depends on placebo construction | W4 already studies role/accuracy variation; W7 establishes context effects. Residual sensitivity to semantically reviewed, token-matched control alternatives is unresolved here | Predeclare original and reviewed control versions, same style/current evidence, compare paired replacement effects. A precise shift across controls supports control dependence | Equivalent effects across independently validated controls within the declared bound | Repetition, instructions, position, semantic distance; two extra controls ×48 proposed cases =96 additional generations | High if merely prompt sensitivity; stronger if a reproducible measurement boundary survives multiple styles/models |
| H2: Ambiguous speaker binding increases historical misattribution | W1/W8 concern speaker grounding; no absence claim about other coreference work | A separate approved explicit-name versus ambiguous-referent diagnostic, balanced history/topic/length; reproducible history-by-binding interaction supports hypothesis | Precise absence of interaction or equal improvement without history | Name tokenization, syntax and ordinary coreference difficulty; 2 binding variants ×2 H levels ×48 =192 diagnostic generations | High: coreference is established; contribution requires persona-specific residual beyond non-personal controls |
| H3: Relevant old facts compete differently from equally presented irrelevant history | W6 covers updates; W7 covers distractors; W9 shows personal-context interference in another endpoint | Cross relevance/time relation in fixed-format history, then fact-equated prose/transcript diagnostic under shared current evidence; errors track content/time even when format is held fixed | No content/time effect within precision, or effect explained solely by position/length | Truth validity, answer competition, assertion count, discourse coherence; 2 relevance ×2 temporal strata ×2 formats ×48 =384 diagnostic generations | High for generic stale-memory errors; potential value is better separation of content and presentation |
| H4: Fidelity and reliability can vary independently across style axes | W2/W5 cover consistency/quality or persona fidelity; the joint construct distinction needs direct tests here | Independently vary brevity/formality/lexical preferences with blind separate annotations; fidelity shifts with stable reliability, or vice versa, support dissociation | Strong consistent coupling across axes/endpoints with adequate coverage contradicts the proposed dissociation | Evaluator style bias, cap-induced censoring, unsupported assertions; core proposed480 generations plus annotation effort | Moderate-to-high: separating constructs is not itself new; a robust dissociation/boundary may contribute |
| H5: Transcript continuation inflates some apparent historical-error rates | W2 recognizes multiple quality dimensions; P0 shows continuation but no causal persona finding | Frozen first-reply versus full-transcript sidecar scores, independently annotated units, same raw pool; systematic H-dependent discrepancies support unit sensitivity | Human-valid first-unit and full-output conclusions agree within a declared bound | Censoring, ambiguous role markers, scoring coverage; no extra generation if pilot raw pool retained, substantial annotation cost | High as a general parser observation; stronger only with an independently reproducible, condition-dependent measurement failure |

Do not pursue all diagnostics as an unbudgeted factorial expansion. Select a small, justified hypothesis family after controls are valid, retain negative results, and reserve untouched confirmation material. Existing literature already studies persona consistency, personalization, context interference, temporal memory and real-person simulation. Potential novelty must come from stronger identification or a reproducible new empirical boundary, not the project name, CPU setting, Qwen model choice or factorial terminology.

## Verification limitations

Nine representative studies were checked against primary records and relevant paper sections; their experiments were not reproduced. The matrix is not a systematic review, and unverified implementation details are not filled from memory. Two guessed arXiv HTML routes returned 404; versioned PDF/abstract sources were then used. No statement that a proposed gap is established novelty is justified by this matrix.

**Assessment:** the v2 implementation is a reproducible engineering improvement, not an established methodological contribution. It eliminates a candidate-pool coverage failure, preserves the original wrapper and separates three history comparisons. These are necessary controls, not evidence of scientific novelty. Neither a different small model nor exact token equality establishes a new method. The diagnostic generalization of an old city still changes informativeness and response pragmatics; it does not identify a pure stale-token mechanism.

A stronger prospective contribution would require showing, on separately frozen material and with real semantic review, that conclusions change systematically across valid control families, or that a persona-specific reliability effect survives length, topic, temporal and pragmatic alternatives. Precise null/equivalence results could falsify those hypotheses. Fidelity must remain a separate outcome. The earlier H1/H3 proposals should now cite W10–W12 and carry **high novelty risk** until broader literature coverage and evidence justify a narrower claim. No P1 run, new algorithm, protocol amendment or novelty claim follows from these tokenizer observations.

Three particularly relevant primary bibliographic records and abstracts were additionally checked:

- **Principled Personas: Defining and Measuring the Intended Effects of Persona Prompting on Task Performance** — Pedro Henrique Luz de Araujo, Paul Röttger, Dirk Hovy and Benjamin Roth; EMNLP 2025, DOI [10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-main.1364](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1364/). The abstract evaluates persona advantage, robustness to irrelevant attributes and fidelity across nine models/27 tasks. This directly limits novelty claims based on detecting unintended persona cues. Detailed model-size claims in the supplied audit were not reverified here.
- **One Persona, Many Cues, Different Results: How Sociodemographic Cues Impact LLM Personalization** — Franziska Weeber, Vera Neplenbroek, Jan Batzner and Sebastian Padó; ACL 2026, DOI [10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.2079](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2079/). The abstract compares six cues, seven models and four tasks and reports sensitivity of conclusions to cue choice. Wrapper preservation and multiple control realizations are therefore supported methodological precautions, not original discoveries.
- **When Personalization Misleads: Understanding and Mitigating Hallucinations in Personalized LLMs** — Zhongxiang Sun, Yi Zhan, Chenglei Shen, Weijie Yu, Xiao Zhang, Ming He and Jun Xu; Findings of ACL 2026, DOI [10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.395](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.395/). Its abstract explicitly studies history-aligned factual distortion and introduces PFQABench/FPPS. Generic personalization-induced factual errors are already studied; no evidence from our tokenization run proves a distinct phenomenon.

This additional check covers metadata and abstracts, not an independent verification of all nine studies or numerical claims in the supplied audit. The existing novelty conclusion remains conservative. The final delivery record preserves that input's hash and distinguishes concurrent file/index changes from this run's actions.
