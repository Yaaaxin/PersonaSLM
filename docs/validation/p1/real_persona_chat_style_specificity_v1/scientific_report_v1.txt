PersonaSLM: controlled real-speaker Style specificity study
2026-10-19 | Researcher: Yaxin ZHENG | DEVELOPMENT / EXPLORATORY

Outcome
All 64 prospectively allocated responses were obtained: eight development
speakers, two new conversational contexts each, four matched arms. The correct
speaker's numeric Style description did not demonstrate a stable advantage over
mismatched Style or a length-only instruction on the registered seven-feature
surface proxy. Small positive full-sample estimates changed sign under
leave-one-speaker-out sensitivity. This is not evidence of equivalence, absence
of genuine persona effects, or validated semantic fidelity. The proxy itself
has weak human-to-human speaker discrimination in an explicitly post-hoc check.

What was compared
NONE: no explicit Style or length instruction.
CORRECT: focal speaker's five numeric marker rates plus focal length target.
MISMATCH: another speaker's five rates, retaining the same focal length target.
LENGTH: focal length target only.
All arms shared the same system, query and six real preceding dialogue turns;
H=1 and F=0 throughout. This tests incremental numeric Style beyond common
history, not the full S/F/H factorial, and NONE is not free of implicit style
cues. The original 64-generation S/F/H pilot is unchanged.

Two earlier distinct-partner dialogues supplied separate prompted source1 and
unprompted reference source2 evidence. Targets came from a later third-partner
dialogue, excluding all prior pilot target dialogues. Two six-turn contexts
were nonoverlapping. Parseable timestamps were required; NaT was not chronology.
The source-only donor derangement excluded shared dialogue/participant sets.
The 58 reserved validation speakers were not opened for target-content tuning.
Participant-level manifests, prompts, references and outputs remain private.

Controls and limits
CORRECT and MISMATCH had identical native input-token counts in all 16 contexts.
Their mean input length was 341.25 tokens, versus 291.25 for LENGTH and 277.25 for
NONE. No fake neutral padding was used. Output budgets were identical (96
tokens), but realized lengths were not forced: post-generation truncation was
used only for the registered sensitivity, never to alter raw responses.
Seven uninstructed character proportions excluded the explicit length
coordinate, but remain associated with topic, orthography, short-text noise and
the instructed markers. They are not a validated pure-style representation.

Post-hoc measurement diagnostics (not prospectively registered outcomes)
After the partial54 analysis, a source1-to-source2 human check was specified
without changing the primary analysis. Only4/8 real speakers had positive own-
versus-donor margins, mean+0.001387513, leave-one-out range[-0.002379405,
+0.004763759]. This weak positive-control discrimination prevents interpreting
the model estimates as a strong test of genuine speaker fidelity.
Normalized outputs were identical for CORRECT/MISMATCH in8/16 contexts,
CORRECT/LENGTH in6/16, and all four arms in4/16. These are output-insensitivity
observations, not human ratings, evidence of semantic correctness, or proof of
one causal mechanism. Lexical overlap and marker adherence remain diagnostics,
not Persona Fidelity. In particular, improving this metric is not itself a
justified algorithmic objective.

Manuscript-ready evidence statement
In a paired developmental four-arm study on eight RealPersonaChat speakers,
correct numeric Style prompts produced small positive length-excluded surface
margin differences relative to mismatched and length-only controls, but both
contrasts changed sign under leave-one-speaker-out sensitivity. A generic
length instruction shortened outputs, while a post-hoc human-reference check
showed weak speaker discrimination by the proxy. These observations motivate
semantic construct validation rather than claims of genuine speaker fidelity.
