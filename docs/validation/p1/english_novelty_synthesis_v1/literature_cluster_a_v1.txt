PersonaSLM: primary-source evidence notes, cluster A, version 1
Verified 2026-10-19. Research documentation: English.

Scope and provenance
This is a bounded literature assessment, not new experimental evidence. The three requested identifiers resolve to the requested works; no identifier/title mismatch was found. Official proceedings PDFs, the Nature article/Methods through its PMC publication copy, and the publisher-hosted Nature supplement were inspected. Additional formal competitors were inspected through their proceedings papers and relevant appendices. This is targeted reading, not a claim that all global sources were collected today or that a daily scheduler ran. No private PersonaSLM mappings, participant records, held-out material or ratings were opened; no model inference or Git mutation was performed. The reported cumulative generation count remains 988/1200; this task adds zero generations. The parent agent maintains the research development log.

1. Persistent Personas? Role-Playing, Instruction Following, and Safety in Extended Interactions
Pedro Henrique Luz de Araujo, Michael A. Hedderich, Ali Modarressi, Hinrich Schuetze, and Benjamin Roth. EACL 2026, long papers, pp. 5329-5359. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2026.eacl-long.246. Formally published, not merely a preprint.
Identity: https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.246/
Methods/evidence: https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.246.pdf
Version link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12775 ; submitted 2025-12-14, latest displayed v2 2026-01-20. Same authors/title; count once, not as two independent studies.

Evidence capsule (main sections 3-7; appendices C, D, F, I): Eight fictional RoleBench characters and a no-persona baseline undergo generated dialogues exceeding 100 rounds. Persona-directed questions and PRISM-derived goal-oriented queries are distinct conditions; authentic query origins do not make generated histories authentic speaker records. Seven instruction models comprise Gemma3 4B/27B, Qwen3 4B/30B-A3B, Nemotron 8B/49B, and Gemini2.5-Flash. Outcomes include character knowledge/style, BFI consistency, RoleBench instruction quality, IFBench constraints, and XSTest refusals. Atla Selene Mini judges most endpoints; IFBench uses task constraints. Persona fidelity deteriorates, especially during goal-oriented interaction; larger models mitigate but do not eliminate drift. Appendix D's single-author 250-item check yields substantially different agreement across domains: general-instruction kappa 0.12, persona-specific 0.44, refusal 0.96; dialogue ratings agree exactly 64%. Appendix F uses 1,000 bootstrap trials. Appendix I reports approximately 700 GPU-hours on H100 servers. These are fictional-role, long-dialogue results with domain-dependent judge validity, not authentic-speaker replication or CPU efficiency evidence.

PersonaSLM assessment (our inference): Broad claims of discovering persona drift, instruction/fidelity conflict, or universal benefit from increasing capacity are unavailable. The meaningful distinction is source-separated authentic-speaker conditioning, not merely another persona label. Our short continuations do not test persistence over 100 rounds. The paper's pooled judge-agreement headline must not substitute for domain-specific validation of our resemblance or coherence judgments.

2. When Personalization Misleads: Understanding and Mitigating Hallucinations in Personalized LLMs
Zhongxiang Sun, Yi Zhan, Chenglei Shen, Weijie Yu, Xiao Zhang, Ming He, and Jun Xu. Findings of ACL 2026, pp. 8041-8060. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.395. Formally published.
Identity: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.395/
Methods/evidence: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.395.pdf
Version link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11000 ; v1 submitted 2026-01-16, only version displayed. Same title/authors; one scientific work.

Evidence capsule (sections 3-5; Table 1; appendices E-G; Ethics): PFQABench pairs 500 LongMemEval user histories with personal questions and topically related but factually irrelevant HotpotQA/2WikiMultiHopQA questions: 1,000 items split equally for training/testing. These benchmark/synthetic histories are not a recruited authentic-speaker cohort. FPPS uses contrastive with/without-history activations, a logistic risk probe, and hard counterfactual restoration, soft factual-direction steering, or their risk-dependent mixture. Backbones are Llama3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B/14B; baselines include retrieval, profile summaries, clustered profiles and recurrent summaries. Qwen2.5-32B judges reference correctness; P-Score measures personal QA, not linguistic fidelity. Table 1's PAG/Qwen7B mixed-steering example changes factual accuracy 27.6% to 84.4%, but personal accuracy 44.0% to 43.2%. Thus preservation is not exact in every setting. The student-learning experiment uses simulated LLM students, not human learning. Appendix G specifies A6000 GPUs and greedy 500-token decoding; CPU/RAM costs and new human judge-agreement evidence are not established in these sections. Intermediate activations are required, limiting direct API deployment. Benchmark construction deliberately selects confounding histories, limiting unconditional population generalization.

PersonaSLM assessment (our inference): This is direct prior art against novelty claims for risk-aware personalization suppression or factuality-preserving activation steering. Implementing a renamed gate is not an algorithmic contribution. A distinct method would need a verified real-speaker failure, comparable evidence access, the hard/soft/mixed alternatives or justified approximations, fidelity assessment and measured CPU overhead. Our available resemblance/relevance scores cannot establish this paper's factual-hallucination mechanism.

3. Training language models to be warm can reduce accuracy and increase sycophancy
Lujain Ibrahim, Franziska Sofia Hafner, and Luc Rocher. Nature 652, 1159-1165 (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10410-0. Accepted 2026-03-12; version of record published 2026-04-29. Formally published and open access.
Identity/main/Methods: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10410-0
Publication copy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13128435/
Publisher supplement: https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10410-0/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10410_MOESM1_ESM.pdf
Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21919 ; v1 2025-07-29, latest displayed v2 2025-07-30. Earlier title: Training language models to be warm and empathetic makes them less reliable and more sycophantic. Author/title evolution identifies one work; the old preprint is not a separate replication.
Access note: direct Nature opens initially returned a redirect/internal error, not evidence of a nonexistent DOI. The official page and supplement were subsequently accessible; no access restriction was bypassed. Cite the supplement's actual headings below: some main-text supplement numbering differs.

Evidence capsule (main Methods; supplement sections 1.1.4, 1.3.1-1.3.2, 2.4-2.5): General warmth, not individual-speaker imitation, is induced using rewritten ShareGPT responses. Models include Llama8B/70B, Qwen32B, Mistral-Small-2409 and GPT-4o. Open models use rank-8 LoRA on two H100s, three for 70B; GPT-4o uses proprietary fine-tuning, with unequal warm/cold learning-rate multipliers. Exact proprietary parameter count is not established. Tests cover TriviaQA, TruthfulQA, MedQA and disinformation, with interpersonal context and incorrect-belief manipulations. Sixty Prolific participants supply 600 warmth judgments on 200 pairs; 75% prefer the warm version on warmth. Separately, two authors validate 235 correctness labels: human kappa 0.917; judge-human 0.814/0.733. This is not wholly external factual adjudication. Refusals are excluded except disinformation refusals counted correct. Warm responses average 734 versus 877 characters; the reported adjusted error increment is +7.43 percentage points, or +6.99 controlling response length (Tables 11-12). Cold-training and prompting controls address alternatives, but stylistic dimensions may co-vary. The study does not identify a unique internal causal mechanism or genuine-speaker fidelity effect.

PersonaSLM assessment (our inference): A general style/reliability trade-off is established prior work; neither novelty nor direct replication follows from our negative S contrast. Length is a demonstrated concern, but attributing this paper's entire result to length would contradict its reported sensitivity analysis. Controlling a post-treatment response length is not equivalent to an experimentally isolated direct effect. Our single-PI judgments must not be equated with the paper's external warmth study. PersonaSLM should distinguish resemblance, warmth, relevance and correctness rather than merge them into one quality score.

4. Additional formal competitor: atomic persona evaluation
Jisu Shin, Juhyun Oh, Eunsu Kim, Hoyun Song, and Alice Oh. Spotting Out-of-Character Behavior: Atomic-Level Evaluation of Persona Fidelity in Open-Ended Generation. Findings of ACL 2025, pp. 26312-26332. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-acl.1349.
Identity: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1349/
Methods/evidence: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1349.pdf

Evidence capsule (sections 3-5; Table 3; appendices A.3-A.5): Fifteen assigned Big-Five trait profiles, not authentic people, generate interviews, essays and social posts. Twelve models include base/tuned Llama3-8B and Mistral7B, Llama70B-Instruct, GPT and Claude variants. Sentence units are scored/filtered by GPT-4o; atomic accuracy, within-response consistency and cross-generation distribution consistency distinguish fluctuations hidden by whole-response averages. Filtering removes units without sufficient personality information. Six native-level English annotators rank 250 sentence pairs; trait-specific Kendall tau is 0.67-0.76 and Fleiss kappa 0.74-0.96. Pair selection requires differing model scores. Appendix A.3 documents differing generation limits across model families; hardware-hours/CPU costs were not established in inspected implementation sections. The authors acknowledge evaluator bias and loss of whole-response context. Sentence-level trait-expression agreement does not validate authentic-speaker likeness or temporal truth, and selected-pair agreement need not transfer to unfiltered conversational responses.

PersonaSLM assessment (our inference): Fine-grained persona inconsistency and limitations of a single holistic score are not new contributions by themselves. This framework is a relevant comparator only when trait targets are independently justified; inventing Big-Five profiles for our speakers would change the construct. Applicability and discarded-unit coverage must accompany any prospective adaptation. No such adaptation or human validation was executed here.

5. Additional formal competitor: personalization safety/utility
Anvesh Rao Vijjini, Somnath Basu Roy Chowdhury, and Snigdha Chaturvedi. Exploring Safety-Utility Trade-Offs in Personalized Language Models. NAACL 2025 long papers, pp. 11316-11340. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-long.565.
Identity: https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.565/
Methods/evidence: https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.565.pdf

Evidence capsule (sections 3-6; appendices A.1-A.3, B.7): System prompts specify 31 demographic user identities, not recruited speakers or longitudinal biographies. Models span Mistral7B, Llama8B/13B/70B, Mixtral8x7B and proprietary systems, with training-stage comparisons including OLMo7B. Utility uses MMLU/GSM8K extraction and MBPP tests; safety uses a pretrained harmful-response classifier rather than newly validated speaker-fidelity ratings. Personalization bias measures variation across identities. Identity effects vary by model, sometimes in opposite directions. Identity-conditioned DPO reduces reported bias 3.60 to 3.17; prompt defenses can lower bias while reducing overall utility. The authors explicitly caution that poor base capability complicates causal attribution to instruction tuning. Implementation uses up to four 48GB A6000s; open models have three runs, API models generally one. Appendix A exposes regex-dependent answer extraction. Selected identity sets, task choice and prompt selection limit inference. Main-section P6 versus appendix P14 naming should be checked against code before reproducing the chosen prompt.

PersonaSLM assessment (our inference): Neither demographic personalization bias nor training-stage differences are available as broad novelty claims. This work personalizes an assistant to a user; PersonaSLM instead asks a model to continue as a real speaker. Confusing these directions would misstate overlap. Our Base/Instruct comparison can support a bounded configuration comparison, not a pure training-cause or model-size conclusion.

Cross-paper decision implications (our scientific assessment)
1. STOP any claim that PersonaSLM first discovers personalization-induced unreliability, persona/instruction trade-offs, atomic fidelity evaluation, or risk-conditioned suppression. The cited published methods are substantive prior art, not title-only resemblance.
2. GO on a bounded empirical contribution if the existing evidence supports it: separate authentic-speaker S/F/H source availability; ordinal, applicability-aware human assessments; negative and heterogeneous effects; and measured native CPU costs. This is a candidate contribution boundary, not proof of publication readiness or literature-wide uniqueness.
3. Keep three distinctions explicit: source-provision intervention versus latent style mechanism; conditional reference correctness versus real-speaker fidelity; and model-configuration comparison versus isolated instruction-training causality. None of these papers supplies missing PersonaSLM labels or independent speakers.
4. The next high-information analysis is the already-generated, model-blinded comparison once genuine review exists, together with preserved missingness and computational costs. Do not spend validation reserve merely reproducing generic warmth or demographic-prompt effects. A new algorithm should follow a replicated, semantically meaningful failure and survive comparisons with existing retrieval/profile baselines and FPPS-type controls where technically applicable.
5. Reading and identifier verification were completed; the papers' results and released code were not independently reproduced. No claims are made about uninspected preprint-to-publication changes beyond verified metadata, undisclosed proprietary parameter counts, absent dataset permissions, or a complete global novelty search.
