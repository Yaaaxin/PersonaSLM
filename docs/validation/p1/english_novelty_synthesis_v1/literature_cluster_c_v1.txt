Verified technical capsule:
MRBench uses characters from ten English and six Chinese novels, not authentic speakers. MRPrompt is a single-pass, prompt-only combination of structured persona facets and cue-driven activation with temporal/epistemic boundaries. Twelve backbones include Qwen3-0.6B. Eight MREval metrics cover anchoring, selecting, bounding and enactment, including anonymization and counterfactual/ablation controls. Qwen3-0.6B aggregate scores are 6.91/7.00/7.29 for Base/Card/MRPrompt; “Base” denotes a prompting baseline, NOT a Base checkpoint. GPT-4.1-mini scoring is calibrated against 800 judgments from one bilingual annotator; a separate post-hoc 100-instance study uses four raters and another judge. The latter reports single-rater ICC 0.6012 and GPT-4.1-mini versus human-mean Spearman 0.3938. Thus human calibration exists, but agreement is not perfect. No train/development/test partition is used for this evaluation benchmark. All inference uses APIs; hardware and GPU-hours are unavailable. Token and latency comparisons do not establish CPU-matched efficiency or rule out every prompt-length effect. Source code is linked, but not executed here.

PersonaSLM inference:

our earlier 0.6B backbone, but not validation of real-speaker S/F/H separation.
A future comparison must distinguish profile information, prompt instructions,
and token budget; do not borrow its human calibration as our own.

Source-time validity is substantive prior art, not equivalent to merely sorting dialogue dates.

Verified technical capsule:
PPA extracts historical personal-knowledge triples, verbalizes them, and stores them with persona facts. A context-only draft becomes the SentenceBERT retrieval query; the retrieved memories guide a second GPT-3.5 generation. Top-k is five, cosine threshold 0.2. The evaluation uses LLM-generated multi-session dialogues, averaging 11.9 sessions, not authentic participant histories. Baselines include DirectGen, DialogRetr, BoB and SimOAP. Outcomes are C-Score, persona-fact overlap, n-gram entropy and ROUGE. Table 1 reports PPA/DirectGen C-Score 0.456/0.221 and Persona-F1 0.146/0.092, but ROUGE 0.182/0.241. Context-query and memory-representation ablations support the proposed mechanism within that setup. No genuine human evaluation, confidence interval, or CPU/peak-RAM benchmark was located in the inspected methods/appendices. Two online generations plus prior extraction are not compute-equivalent to one-pass generation merely because the backbone matches. Appendix A contains output placeholders; their existence motivates a control, not a claim that this paper's result is contaminated. The results do not independently validate real-speaker linguistic fidelity.

PersonaSLM inference:
Draft-then-retrieve-then-revise is an existing RQ4 baseline, not our invention.
A fair future experiment needs a two-call no-retrieval control and matched
information access, plus human quality assessment. Selecting from our tiny
history pool may not discriminate retrieval methods meaningfully.

Read locators: Experimental Setup, Results, Limitations; Tables 1, 3-5;

Verified technical capsule:
The main experiment uses 180 synthetic PersonaMem-v2 four-option questions, at most one per persona, with approximately 32k-token histories. Sentence chunks are ranked by text-embedding-3-small request similarity, budgeted by characters, and restored chronologically. GPT-5.6-terra and Claude Opus 5.5 select answers; summaries are request-blind. At 7,331 characters (4.7% of full history), retrieval accuracy is 40.6%/54.4%, versus full-history 43.3%/54.4%. Paired bootstrap differences are -2.8 points [-10.0,3.9] and 0.0 [-7.8,7.8]. Failure to detect a difference is NOT a prespecified equivalence result. The reported resource saving is input text, not measured CPU latency, peak RAM, or end-to-end cost including indexing. Parsing failures get one retry. No human semantic evaluation or open-ended speaker imitation is established. Appendix D discloses earlier answerability, parsing, length and dependence confounds. Request-aware retrieval versus request-blind summarization and single runs limit causal interpretation. Ask-to-forget results also caution against assuming that retaining preference evidence preserves the user's latest instructions.

PersonaSLM inference:
This blocks novelty claims for generic relevance filtering or a universal
five-percent rule. It does not establish a twenty-fold CPU saving for our
already-short history or reliable open-ended persona generation. A meaningful
replication requires sufficient history diversity and fixed quality-loss margins.
