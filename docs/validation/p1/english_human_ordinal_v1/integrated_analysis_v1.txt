PersonaSLM: integrated exploratory human-ordinal analysis of English S/F/H dialogue
Version 1; 19 October 2026
Status: observed developmental results; retrospective instrument amendment;
independently reproduced arithmetic, NOT independent human or experimental validation.

Scientific result

In the completed English real-speaker experiment, the current Style intervention
did not establish a general benefit in principal-researcher-perceived relevance,
coherence, or linguistic resemblance. Facts and History had different effects
across these domains and across ordinal thresholds. Correct Style occasionally
outperformed mismatched or pooled Style, but did not produce a positive paired
net advantage over no Style when Facts and History were both present. These
findings concern this specific conditioning implementation and developmental
sample, not the general effectiveness of speaker personalization.

Design, instrument deviation, and estimands

The archived experiment contains five actual English-speaking development
speakers, two conversational contexts each, and ten conditions per context:
C0-C7 for the S/F/H factorial, plus M_STYLE and N_STYLE. There are 80 factorial
responses and 20 style-control responses from the existing Qwen3-0.6B Q8_0
configuration. S supplies four aggregate linguistic rates, not an exhaustive
representation of speaker style; F supplies dated professional self-reports,
not independently verified biography; H supplies earlier natural dialogue.
The source-provision factors are experimentally controlled, but historical
dialogue naturally contains stylistic and factual information. Their effects
must not be interpreted as perfectly isolated latent psychological constructs.

The principal researcher affirmed 99 genuine manual judgments. The actual
instrument used ordered categories 0-5, with higher values better, 0 a valid
lowest category, and 5 the highest. Intermediate verbal anchors were not
established. The original frozen instrument specified 0-3 plus applicability
rules and was not passed. The actual scale was clarified retrospectively;
original submissions, original validation errors, and original rubric remain
unchanged. This is a documented alternate-instrument deviation, not a retroactive
prospective registration or a recoding of the original ratings.

Analysis rules and source hashes were locked after collection and admissibility
inspection, but before the authorized condition-map join. Three primary domains
are analyzed: relevance, coherence, and linguistic resemblance. One allocated
C1 response has no submitted primary judgment and remains UNAVAILABLE. All
other 99 responses have three admissible ordinal ratings. Secondary history,
temporal, behavioral, and completeness ratings, claim counts/evidence, confidence,
and other unresolved fields are preserved but excluded from outcome analysis.
They are not repaired or used to infer factual accuracy.

For each main factor, ordinal wins, ties, and losses compare factor-on with
factor-off within the same speaker, context, and other-factor stratum. There
are 40 allocated pairs per main effect and 39 observed pairs. The three style
controls each have ten fully observed pairs. Net advantage is P(win)-P(loss),
not a mean rating difference. Full-design summaries weight the five speakers
equally and the two contexts/within-context strata equally. The one unavailable
category is enumerated over 0-5 separately for each domain. Minima and maxima
are logical missing-outcome bounds, not imputations, confidence intervals,
population estimates, or simultaneous cross-domain bounds.

Full-design paired main effects

Each interval below is the net-advantage bound for all five speakers. Units are
probabilities; multiplying by 100 gives percentage points. Negative values mean
more ordinal losses than wins, not a cardinal reduction in a quality score.

Domain                  S                 F                 H
relevance               [-0.150,-0.125]   [-0.075,-0.025]   [ 0.000, 0.050]
coherence               [-0.100,-0.075]   [-0.050, 0.000]   [-0.075,-0.025]
linguistic_resemblance  [-0.150,-0.125]   [ 0.075, 0.125]   [-0.025, 0.025]

Thus S has a negative paired net direction in all three domains under every
allowed missing-category completion. F has a positive linguistic-resemblance
direction but a negative relevance direction. H does not establish a positive
linguistic-resemblance direction and has a negative coherence direction. These
are finite-sample descriptions, not calibrated significance claims.

Complete ordinal threshold profiles and interactions

For every ordered cut k=1,...,5, Y_k=1(rating >= k). All seven factorial
contrasts use 2*mean(product of selected +/-1 factor codes * Y_k), with equal
context/speaker weights. S, F, and H correspond to condition-number bits 1,
2, and 4. Factorial-scaled interactions are not unscaled differences-in-
differences. No cut is designated a success threshold, and cuts are not averaged
into an equal-interval score.

each cell the five entries are k=1,2,3,4,5. P means strictly positive under all
allowed completions; N means strictly negative; ZP means a nonnegative bound
including zero; ZN means a nonpositive bound including zero. These symbols
describe missing-completion sensitivity, not statistical significance. Exact
bounds, all six completion profiles, and speaker summaries are in artifact A,
estimates.domains.<domain>.threshold_factorial.<effect>.<k>.

S is nonpositive over the entire relevance profile. Its linguistic-resemblance
profile, however, is positive at k=4, with a bound [0.025,0.050], and negative
at the other four cuts. Therefore a negative paired net effect does not imply
a uniformly worse distribution at every ordinal threshold. SF is nonnegative
at all cuts in all domains, but this does not establish universal synergy or
restore a general S benefit. SH, FH, and SFH change direction across cuts. For
example, SH on coherence is positive at cuts 1-3 and negative at cuts 4-5.
These crossing profiles preclude reduction to one favorable interaction claim.

Speaker heterogeneity and missingness

Complete-speaker analyses retain four speakers for the factorial/main-effect
estimands because one speaker has an unavailable C1 rating. They estimate a
different subset from the full-design bounds. For S on linguistic resemblance,
the four sorted speaker net values are [-0.500,-0.250,-0.125,0.125], with mean
-0.1875 and leave-one-speaker-out mean range [-0.291667,-0.083333]. For S on
relevance, the corresponding values are [-0.625,-0.250,0,0.125], with mean
-0.1875 and leave-one-speaker-out range [-0.291667,-0.041667]. The negative
subset direction is not attributable solely to one of these four speakers,
but the small dependent sample does not support a calibrated population CI.

For C7-C6, the five sorted speaker net values are [-1,0,0,0.5,0.5] in each
domain; a zero aggregate masks opposing outcomes. For C7-N_STYLE they are
[0,0,0,0,0.5], so removing the sole positive speaker reduces the mean to zero.
For C7-M_STYLE they are [0,0,0,0.5,0.5], with a leave-one-speaker-out mean
range [0.125,0.250]. These ranges diagnose influence; they are not CIs.

Changing the estimand can reverse the apparent direction. For F on relevance
at k=4, the complete-four-speaker point is +0.0625, whereas the full-five-speaker
logical bound is [-0.025,0]. This is not an arithmetic inconsistency: the latter
retains the fifth speaker's observed information and bounds the missing cell.
The complete-case estimate must not be presented as a full-cohort point.

Joint quality and latency

The same ordinal pair outcomes are cross-tabulated against exact faster/equal/
slower signs of archived completion wall latency. All 100 technical outputs
exist, including the response with an unavailable human judgment. Across the
40 main-factor technical pairs, factor-on is slower in 75% for S, 85% for F,
and 100% for H. No exact latency ties occur. This is a descriptive sign count,
not a practically meaningful speed threshold or a controlled mediation result.

For H, all quality outcomes therefore occupy the slower column. Full-design
win/tie/loss bounds are:

Domain                  Win, slower       Tie, slower       Loss, slower
relevance               [.300,.325]       [.400,.425]       [.275,.300]
coherence               [.225,.250]       [.475,.500]       [.275,.300]
linguistic_resemblance  [.225,.250]       [.525,.550]       [.225,.250]

These bounds preserve uncertainty about the unavailable category; endpoints
of different cells are not freely combinable. H's extra latency did not buy
a uniform perceived-quality advantage. For C7-C6 in each domain, both wins are
slower, both losses are faster, and the six ties comprise one faster and five
slower responses. For C7-M_STYLE, the two wins split one faster/one slower and
the eight ties split three faster/five slower. For C7-N_STYLE, the sole win is
faster and the nine ties split four faster/five slower. The complete nine-cell
joint summaries for every factor/domain/control remain in artifact A.

Latency measures are CPU-backend elapsed completion time and remain sensitive
to output length, execution order, system load, and the archived safe restart.
They are not CPU energy or per-condition peak RAM. No ordinal rating-per-second
ratio, cardinal quality-cost utility, or causal mediation is computed.

Research-question coverage and limitations

RQ1 receives exploratory evidence for S/F/H main and interaction effects on
three PI-perceived constructs, under this source-provision design. It does not
establish general behavioral Persona Fidelity, stable speaker identity
reproduction, or effects for the wider speaker/model population. Existing
context can itself expose speaker cues, and H is not devoid of S/F content.

RQ2 receives limited evidence about H-associated perceived relevance/coherence
and latency. Actual factual and temporal reliability remain unanswered because
the corresponding secondary applicability and evidential requirements were
not met. Language resemblance is not a substitute for either construct.

RQ3 is not answered by a single model configuration. RQ4 is not answered:
no retrieval, candidate-ranking, or LoRA comparison is present in this analysis.
No algorithmic novelty or validated failure-repair mechanism is claimed.

The evaluator is the principal researcher and is not independent of the
project. Human authorship and condition-blinded annotation were affirmed by the
PI; the package masked displayed conditions. Verified absence of prior exposure,
double-blind review, independent inter-rater reliability, complete review-date/
aid histories, and a full competence attestation were not established. The
unanchored intermediate ordinal categories and retrospective scale clarification
limit interpretation. Genuine judgments are not equivalent to validated
measurement or independent confirmation. No new human labels were generated.

Five speakers from a dependent meeting setting and ten contexts cannot support
responses-as-independent inference. Missing bounds, speaker influence, and
measurement limitations are distinct uncertainty sources. No p-values,
calibrated confidence intervals, or confirmatory significance claims are made.
Existing condition distributions, including all six category counts and
missingness, are reported in artifact A rather than collapsed into means.

Next-stage decision: conditional RQ3 configuration comparison, NOT executed here

The selected next candidate is a prospectively frozen, same-family Base/Instruct
comparison using the English factorial task design: a finite maximum of 200
generations, including 40 style-control generations. This would examine whether
the observed pattern is stable across two deployment configurations and would
advance the Base/Instruct component of RQ3. It would not isolate a pure training
effect if templates, special tokens, stopping behavior, or other implementation
details differ; it would not establish a model-size effect. Reusing developmental
contexts is explicitly not independent validation. Model identity, licensing,
configuration, prompts, analysis, resource allocation, and RAM safeguards must
be frozen and verified before any such execution. Actual human endpoints remain
dependent on genuine new evaluation, not automated substitution.

This candidate was preferred operationally over expanding an as-yet-unqualified
new-speaker cohort, while preserving that route as a separate future study.
Retrieval/algorithm development was deprioritized because these results do not
yet identify a semantically validated, technically differentiated failure-repair
target. This decision is prospective research direction, not a completed model
comparison, a universal dataset infeasibility claim, or an innovation result.

Resource accounting for this analytical stage is unchanged: zero new model
generations and zero new inference seconds; cumulative 788/1200 generations
and 4485.684496/72000 completion seconds (rounded from 4485.684495999997).
Of the 412 remaining generations, 160 remain reserved for independent validation.
Executing the entire proposed 200-generation comparison would leave 212,
including that reserve; it has not occurred as part of the present analysis.
